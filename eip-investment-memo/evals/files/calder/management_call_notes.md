## Company background

Calder Water Infrastructure was founded in 2011 in Harrisburg, PA by Dana Whitfield and
her business partner Ron Calder (retired 2021, no remaining economic interest). The
company provides inspection, rehabilitation and condition assessment services for
municipal water and wastewater collection systems — primarily trenchless pipe lining
(CIPP), CCTV inspection, and hydraulic modelling support for capital planning.

Headcount is 214 FTEs across four operating yards: Harrisburg PA (HQ), Akron OH,
Richmond VA, and a yard opened in Charlotte NC in Q2 2025. Field crews are ~78% of
headcount.

Whitfield: "We are not a construction company that happens to do water. Everything we
own is water-specific — the liner trucks, the CCTV rigs, the modelling team. That's why
we win the assessment work, and the assessment work is what tells us where the
rehabilitation dollars go next."

## Demand drivers

Arroyo walked through the funding picture. Municipal water capex is driven by consent
decrees (EPA enforcement on combined sewer overflow), state revolving fund allocations,
and the residual IIJA money still flowing through state programs. He noted the IIJA
tailwind is "real but finite" and that they model it as flat from 2027.

Raman added that roughly 60% of their revenue comes from municipalities operating under
an active EPA consent decree, which she characterised as non-discretionary spend: "The
city doesn't get to decide not to do it. They get to decide who does it and in what order."

## Contract structure

- **MSA / on-call work:** ~64% of FY2025 revenue. Multi-year master service agreements,
  typically 3–5 years, renewed by task order. Whitfield said renewal rate on MSAs coming
  up for rebid has been "we've lost two in fourteen years."
- **Bid-build project work:** ~29%. Competitively bid, lumpier, lower margin.
- **Assessment & modelling:** ~7%. Highest margin, acts as a lead generator into the
  rehabilitation work.

## Customers

Top customer is the City of Akron (consent decree since 2009), ~19% of FY2025 revenue,
under an MSA running through 2029. Second is Pennsylvania American Water at ~13%.
Third is Henrico County VA at ~11%. Roughly 90 active customers in total.

Arroyo acknowledged concentration: "Akron is a big number and we know it. But we're in
year sixteen with them and we've been through three utility directors."

## Operations and labour

Raman said the binding constraint is licensed CIPP crew leads, not demand. They run a
in-house training program and have moved to a retention bonus structure. Voluntary crew
turnover was 14% in FY2025, down from 22% in FY2023. Wage inflation for field labour is
running 5–6% annually; roughly 71% of MSAs carry annual escalators tied to ENR or CPI.

The Charlotte yard is not yet at target utilisation — Raman said it is "running about
55% against a 75% target" and expects to reach target in mid-2027.

## Growth plan under Halstead Ridge

Nakamura outlined the sponsor's plan: (i) fill Charlotte to target utilisation,
(ii) open two additional yards in the Southeast (Nashville and Atlanta identified, neither
under lease), (iii) two to three tuck-in acquisitions of regional inspection firms.
Nakamura said they have "a pipeline of eight targets" but confirmed none are under LOI.
